## Carried forward from plan 1 (from its reviews)

**Plan 2 must start with these:**
- `PUT /products/{id}/variants` deletes and re-inserts variants (new ids, stock reset). Once orders reference variants, change it to upsert by attributes, or forbid replacing a variant that is referenced.
- No upper bounds on price, stock or weight yet. Add caps, and use checked arithmetic for kobo totals.
- Switch the three `users ... FOR UPDATE` locks (OTP issue, code redeem, store create) to `FOR NO KEY UPDATE` together. Global lock order: users → stores → products → variants → access_codes.
- Add graceful shutdown before payment webhooks arrive.
- Path ids: never use raw `Path<Uuid>`; use `Path<String>` + `parse_id`. Bodies use `AppJson<T>`.

**Before any public launch or real SMS provider:**
- Per-IP and per-phone limits on sign-up and sign-in (reverse proxy or app). Without them, anyone can lock a phone's sign-in for an hour, or keep a pending sign-up from verifying.
- The production database role must not own `audit_log`; revoke UPDATE/DELETE/TRUNCATE from the app role.
- DPO notes: sign-up's 409 `phone_taken` reveals whether a number has an account; failed-attempt tables keyed by user/phone need retention.

**Later plans:**
- Plan 8 (erasure): purge must clear or rotate `users.phone_idx` so a deleted number can sign up again; purge `redeem_attempts`, `sign_in_attempts`, old sessions and OTPs; purge `orders.delivery_address_enc` (setting `delivery_address_purged_at`) once the DPO has set its window.
- Upload plan: check that each photo key belongs to the uploading store.
- SMS-provider plan: a throttled resend can return a `verification_id` whose code was never sent; tell the client to wait and retry.
- Smaller polish items (input trimming, pagination and N+1 on store product lists, PATCH can't clear optional fields, empty PATCH writes an audit row).

If the wallet is credited, post the reverse under `("courier_refund", shipment_id)`, and have the accountant confirm.

- Accountant: is delivery vatable at 7.5%, and who is merchant of record for courier delivery.

  - a privacy notice that names couriers;

  - whether a handover code should release after a delay, or have a no-code fallback, since a coerced code releases immediately;
  - whether the 48-hour ship deadline should pause on holidays and Sundays (today it is calendar hours).

## Carried forward from plan 7e-1b

**Go-live (owner, legal, DPO), for review and not as conclusions:**

- **Plug in (owner):** choose and open the identity provider account (candidates: Smile ID, Prembly, Dojah, YouVerify; confirm NIMC licensing first, where it processes data and how long it keeps the selfie). Then write its adapter behind `IdentityProvider` (`server/src/identity/mod.rs`; `FakeIdentity` shows the shape), add its name to `KNOWN_IDENTITY_PROVIDERS` in `config.rs`, set `IDENTITY_PROVIDER`, `IDENTITY_API_KEY` and `IDENTITY_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, and run the provider's sandbox: a good match, a face mismatch, a NIN not found, a minor, and a missing webhook (the poll job). Production refuses to start without a provider and `NIN_INDEX_KEY`, and refuses the fake.
- **Key (owner):** generate `NIN_INDEX_KEY` (`openssl rand -base64 32`), different from `FIELD_KEY` and `INDEX_KEY`, outside the repository. Never rotate it (every verified seller would have to verify again) and back it up with the other keys. Losing it frees every NIN for reuse.
- **DPIA (DPO):** a data protection impact assessment is likely required (biometric selfie match at scale). Do it before go-live.
- **Lawful basis and consent (DPO):** fraud prevention as legitimate interest or contract, plus explicit agreement to the provider's biometric processing, which is sensitive data under NDPA 2023. The notice wording is drafted in 7e-2 and needs legal review.
- **Processor agreement and transfers (DPO):** a data processing agreement with the provider; NDPA cross-border rules if it processes outside Nigeria.
- **NIMC rules (legal):** confirm what a licensed verifier's client may store (we keep a keyed fingerprint, the encrypted verified name and the year of birth).
- **Retention (DPO):** the fingerprint is personal data (pseudonymised). Proposed: deleted with an unbanned account at erasure (part 8, not built, so nothing deletes it yet); kept while a ban stands for a banned account, with a review period the DPO sets. Attempt rows (status, reason code, times) have no purge window yet.
- **Ban blocks fewer NINs than the design said:** a ban blocks the fingerprints of attempts that did not fail. A failed face match is most often someone trying another person's NIN, and blocking it would block the victim. The cost: a banned scammer who only ever failed can try again. Legal and the owner to confirm.
- **Refusals leave no fingerprint,** and a start refused for a banned or used NIN looks the same as a provider refusal (same 409, same wait). The real reason is in `audit_log` by code.
- **Owner notes** on review decisions and revokes are free text stored on `identity_checks.decision_note` (no run of 10 or more digits), visible to the Owner role only and never in the audit log. Decide how long they are kept.
- **Name reads are audited** (`admin.identity_read`); Support sees statuses and reason codes only.
- **Under 18:** goes to the review queue; the owner decides each case (confirmed default). Whether minors may sell at all is a legal question.
- **Existing sellers:** 14 days of grace from the migration, then their stores go `pending` (plot kept, open orders still complete). Announce it before deploy; the owner can change `identity_grace_days` first.
- **Ban now also suspends the user's stores** (`suspended_reason = 'user_banned'`); an unban restores only those, through the normal rules.
- **Provider cost:** `identity_daily_budget` (default 200 provider calls a day) stops starts with 503 `identity_unavailable` and raises the `identity.budget_exhausted` alert. Set the budget from the provider's price. There is no settings screen for the identity numbers yet: they are `platform_settings` columns (`identity_*`).
- **Not built here (7e-2):** the app screens, including the notice, NIN entry, the provider SDK's selfie capture and the refused state.

## Carried forward from plan 7f

- **The real art is the owner's work:** Unreal renders of one square top-down market and one interior per store type, handed over as an art pack (`server/market/README.md`). Until then the app uses its bundled placeholder art, and nothing about publishing is needed.

- **The layout and the art move together:** the pack's `layout.json` and room files must replace the server's `market/` copy and the app's bundled copy in the same release. `art verify` says when the published art's layout and the server's differ. Changing plots moves nobody who already has one.
- **Pictures are JPEG (depth PNG), not WebP or AVIF,** because the Rust image library only writes lossless WebP. Revisit if the bundle size or load time needs it.
- **Square world only:** the app draws an `n x n` grid of square tiles, so a non-square market is refused by `art check`.
- **Go-live check:** `art publish` needs the object store's public bucket and its CDN to serve the `market/` path with the cache header kept; run `art verify` against the real public address once, and check the first view on a mid-range phone over 4G (the design's 1.5 s aim). Not run on a device.
- **Not covered:** how the real depth maps look at their edges on a phone (the design's known risk), and the tool on a full-size render (16384 px takes a lot of memory; it is meant for a developer's machine).

## Carried forward from plan 8

- **DPO and legal review (not legal advice):** the defaults are placeholders the Owner can change in `GET/PATCH /admin/retention`: chat 12 months (longer while a dispute on the order is open), records 6 years, delivery addresses 12 months after an order ends, notices 6 months, stale push devices 90 days, attempt rows 30 days, deletion cooling-off 30 days (7 to 60). The DPO confirms each before launch. The privacy notice must say what is kept, for how long, that a store's team may answer buyers, that an AI provider checks chats, how to download and delete, and that some records stay after deletion and why.
- **Not built, by decision:** the money and order records themselves are never deleted yet. At erasure they lose the person (name, phone, address, handover code, courier phone, payout account number) but the rows stay. Deleting them after the records window needs legal's answer and a ledger design that keeps totals right. Dispute statements are removed after the records window; the dispute row and decision stay.
- **Differences from the design:** retention windows have their own Owner routes and no dashboard form; the current session stays open but gated during the cooling-off, other sessions are signed out; no inbox notice is created about a due deletion (the app shows the date on sign-in); the inventory also covers stores, branches, payout accounts, courier phones and identity names, which the guard test found.
- **Guard test:** `every_table_with_personal_data_has_a_fate` fails when a table that points at a user, or has a personal-looking column, is not listed in `privacy::erase::INVENTORY`. Add the table and its fate there.
- **Addresses** are emptied, not deleted, because an order's delivery option points at one; the empty rows stay.
- **Erasure and the other side:** a deleted person's messages stay for the other party until the chat window ends, shown as "Deleted user". Reviews stay, without photos.
- **Not covered:** the identity provider's and the object store's own retention of selfies and photos, the 72-hour breach notice procedure (an owner action), a dashboard queue of requests, photo files in the export, and a run on a device. The share sheet (saving the export file) has only been tested with a stand-in.
